On the Navier–Stokes Millennium Prize Problem

OpenAI says about 10,000 of its AI agents produced a solution, including a formal Lean proof, to the Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness problem, one of seven Millennium Prize Problems, in 88 hours at a cost of more than $40 million. The claim has been disputed by mathematicians including Terence Tao and an NYU researcher who accuse OpenAI of overstating credit and "fighting dirty" on a problem others were already working on, and CEO Sam Altman says OpenAI faced unfounded plagiarism accusations.

Why it matters: If validated, it would be a landmark demonstration of AI doing original mathematical research, but the credibility fight raises new questions about how AI-assisted results should be verified and credited.
